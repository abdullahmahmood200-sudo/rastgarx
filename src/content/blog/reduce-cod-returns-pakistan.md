Cash on delivery is how most people in Pakistan still prefer to shop online. It builds trust and brings in orders. It also brings in returns: parcels refused at the door, customers who stop picking up the phone, and fake orders that were never meant to be paid for.

Every returned COD order costs you delivery both ways, packaging, stock tied up for days and the ad spend that brought the order in. For many stores, returns are the difference between a profitable month and a loss.

This guide explains why COD returns happen and gives 12 fixes that reduce them, in the order most stores should try them. RastgarX runs performance marketing and automations for ecommerce brands, so keep that in mind as you read. Every fix works whoever sets it up.

## What is a COD return?

A COD return, often called **RTO (return to origin)**, is a cash on delivery parcel that comes back to the seller unpaid. The rider attempts delivery, the customer refuses or cannot be reached, and the courier sends the parcel back and charges you for both trips.

Your **COD return rate** is:

> COD return rate = returned COD orders ÷ dispatched COD orders × 100

If you dispatched 400 COD orders last month and 80 came back, your return rate is 20%.

## Why customers in Pakistan refuse COD parcels

Most refusals come from a handful of causes:

- **Fake or prank orders** placed with no intention to pay.
- **Impulse orders** the customer regrets by the time the rider arrives.
- **Wrong or incomplete addresses and phone numbers,** so the rider cannot find or reach the customer.
- **Slow delivery.** The longer the wait, the more likely the customer has bought elsewhere or changed their mind.
- **The product looks different** from the photos, or the size is wrong.
- **No cash at the door** when the rider arrives unannounced.
- **A misleading ad or offer** that promised a price, gift or discount the order did not include.

Each fix below targets one or more of these causes.

## 12 ways to reduce COD returns in Pakistan

### 1. Confirm every order before dispatch

This is the single most effective fix. When an order is placed, send the customer a WhatsApp message with the product, price and address, and buttons to **Confirm** or **Cancel**. Call the customers who do not reply within a few hours. Dispatch only confirmed orders.

Confirmation removes fake orders, catches wrong addresses and gives impulse buyers a moment to cancel for free, before the parcel costs you anything. ### 2. Check the address and phone number at checkout

Make the checkout ask for a complete address: house number, street, area, city and a nearby landmark. Validate the phone number format so typos are caught before the order is placed. A rider who cannot find the house sends the parcel back.

### 3. Block repeat refusers

Keep a list of phone numbers and addresses that have refused parcels before. Flag new orders that match, and ask those customers for an advance payment or skip COD for them. Some couriers and plugins can share refusal history, so check what yours offers.

### 4. Take a small advance on high-risk orders

For large orders, first-time customers in high-return areas, or numbers with a refusal history, ask for the delivery charge or a small part of the order value in advance through a bank transfer, JazzCash or Easypaisa. A customer who has paid something is far more likely to accept the parcel.

### 5. Reward customers who pay online

Offer free delivery, a small discount or a gift to customers who pay in advance. Every prepaid order is one that cannot be refused for lack of cash. Keep the reward smaller than the cost of an average return.

### 6. Show the product exactly as it arrives

Use real photos of the product, including close-ups of fabric, colour and finish, and a short video where possible. If the parcel looks different from the ad, the customer has a reason to refuse it. For clothing, add model measurements and the size the model is wearing.

### 7. Add a clear size chart

Size problems are a major cause of returns in fashion and footwear. Show a size chart in centimetres and inches on every product page, with a simple way to measure. Answer size questions on WhatsApp before the order ships.

### 8. Ship fast and say when it will arrive

Dispatch confirmed orders the same or next day, and show a realistic delivery estimate on the product page and in the confirmation message. A customer who waits a week has more time to change their mind.

### 9. Tell the customer the day the rider is coming

Send a WhatsApp message with the tracking number when the parcel ships, and another on the day of delivery. Customers who know the rider is coming make sure someone is home with the cash ready.

### 10. Choose couriers by return rate per city

Courier performance varies by city and area. Track your return rate for each courier in each city, and route orders to the courier that delivers best there. A courier with slightly higher charges and fewer failed deliveries is often cheaper overall.

### 11. Make your ads match the order

If the ad shows a price, offer or bundle, the product page and the parcel must match it exactly. Ads that overpromise bring in orders that come back. Check your return rate by campaign and ad: some ads bring buyers, some bring impulse clicks that turn into refusals.

### 12. Track returns by product, city, courier and campaign

You cannot fix what you do not measure. Every week, check your return rate split by:

- **Product.** Fix the photos, size charts or descriptions of the products that come back most.
- **City and area.** Ask for advance payment, or change courier, where returns are highest.
- **Courier.** Move volume to the couriers that deliver best.
- **Ad campaign.** Cut or rework the ads that bring in orders that are later refused.

## How COD returns change your ad profit

Returns quietly raise the cost of every order your ads bring in. Here is a hypothetical example with illustrative numbers:

| | 10% returns | 30% returns |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Orders from ads | 100 | 100 |
| Ad spend | Rs 100,000 | Rs 100,000 |
| Delivered and paid orders | 90 | 70 |
| Ad cost per delivered order | Rs 1,111 | Rs 1,429 |
| Return delivery charges (Rs 250 each) | Rs 2,500 | Rs 7,500 |

Same ads, same spend. The store with 30% returns pays nearly 30% more in ads for every order that is actually paid for, plus three times the return delivery charges.

This is why your break-even ROAS has to include returns. ## Where to start

If you do only three things this month:

1. **Confirm every order on WhatsApp before dispatch,** with a call for anyone who does not reply.
2. **Start a refusal list** and ask for advance payment from repeat refusers.
3. **Track your return rate by product, city, courier and campaign** every week.

These three cover the biggest causes of COD returns and cost very little to set up.
